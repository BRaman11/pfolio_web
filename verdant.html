Brand Identity · Case Study

Verdant Coffee Roasters

Verdant is a coffee roaster committed to delivering sustainably sourced, high-quality coffee beans that elevate both the experience of coffee drinkers and the success of local cafes. They strive to build lasting partnerships, helping cafes thrive while nurturing a better, more sustainable future for the coffee industry as a whole.

RoleBrand & Logo Design
DeliverablesIdentity · Packaging
ToolsIllustrator · Photoshop
Year2025

01 — Brief

Verdant required a brand identity that reflected the values at the core of their business — a genuine commitment to quality and sustainability. From sourcing the finest coffee beans for their clients, to the care taken at every step to ensure their environmental impact remains net positive, the brand needed to communicate intention and integrity.

02 — Process

Discovery

The colour palette was drawn from the Coffea Arabica plant itself — earthy tones of red, brown and green that reflect the natural origins of the product. This grounded, organic palette reinforced the client's commitment to environmental care, a direction established early through moodboard exploration.

A SWOT analysis was conducted to identify gaps in the competitive landscape and define a clear strategic direction for the brand. Each competitor selected shares values with Verdant and operates within the Sydney, NSW region — making them direct competitors.

First Iteration

Various ideas were sketched out, with the strongest ones explored further. The key idea being worked with was creating a lettermark of the "V" that represents the company's main values — environmental preservation and nurturing growth in the cafes they serve. Other imagery experimented with related to coffee: beans, the coffee plant, and the brewing process.

This is the first iteration presented to the client. The coffee bean and plant form the "V" and each represent Verdant's key values — great coffee and sustainable practices.

Revisions

The general consensus was that this first logo was too obvious, and they wanted a more minimal mark. Looking back, I do believe it was too on-the-nose and didn't have much professional appeal for a company that wants to be at the top of their game.

The next iterations worked on much more subtle hints at what Verdant does and stands for — something instantly recognisable at the sight of it on a coffee cup or a sign at the front of a cafe. Ideas here incorporated less obvious but still well-known references. The grounded icon was used as inspiration for the "growth" spouting of a plant.

This was the sketch of the final version. It uses a form that grows on each iteration up. Three steps up were used, not only because this is what is used in the grounded symbol, but each step represents the three major steps in coffee brewing — Drying, Maillard and Development. The colours were then assigned to resemble the range of roasts Verdant offers, going from light roast to dark roast.

Final Design

The final design uses colours that represent nature and the array of coffee roasts that Verdant sell. The fonts were selected for their inviting appeal, yet able to stand out on packaging amongst the various other coffee beans on shelves. The logo strikes the right balance of showing what the company stands for and imagery that catches the eye.
